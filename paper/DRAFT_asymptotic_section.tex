\subsection{The projection variances in closed form}
\label{sec:zeta-closed-form}

Section~3.4 rules out a weight-only quadratic form for \(\zeta_1\). What
it does not rule out, and what does hold, is a cubic one. The ingredient
is already in the literature: Lemma 4 of \cite{huang2020shadows} gives
the exact second moment of two reconstructed Pauli coefficients under
local shadows,
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\hat x_{P}\, \hat x_{Q}\right]
= 3^{\,|\mathrm{supp}(P)\, \cap\, \mathrm{supp}(Q)|}\;
\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\rho\, P\!\ominus\!Q\right),
\]
when \(P\) and \(Q\) agree on every qubit where both are non-identity and
zero otherwise, where \(\hat x_P = \mathrm{Tr}(G P)\) and \(P \ominus Q\)
cancels the shared non-identity factors. We claim no novelty for this.
%% [V-SRC] HKP eq. S52, verified verbatim. Contains the full off-diagonal
%% structure for P != Q, not only the diagonal.

Since \(\mathrm{Tr}(G\rho) = 2^{-n}\sum_s \langle P_s\rangle \hat x_s\),
the first projection variance follows by contraction, and since the
\(k\)-argument kernel of Section~3.1 is multilinear in the snapshots,
every \(\zeta_c\) is a quadratic functional of the same second-moment
matrix. Two consequences are worth recording before we evaluate.
%% [V-CC] contraction verified against the committed kernel at 4e-15.

First, the diagonal \(s = s'\) of that contraction is exactly the
weight-only form of Section~3.4, with \(c_w = 3^w/4^n\). The residual
that form leaves is exactly the off-diagonal sum. The shortfall
identified there is therefore not an open feature of the algebra.
%% [V-CC] free c_w fit to the diagonal recovers 3^w/4^n to 9e-16; the
%% weight-only fit to exact zeta_1 over the nineteen-state family leaves
%% max relative residual 0.122 against the committed 0.123.

Second, because only second moments enter, the projection variances do
not distinguish local Haar single-qubit shadows from local Pauli-basis
shadows: the two ensembles have entrywise identical second-moment
matrices, so \(\zeta_c\) agrees for every \(c\) and every \(k\). The
analysis below therefore covers both.
%% [V-CC] second-moment matrices agree to 1.8e-15; zeta_2 to 8.5e-14;
%% zeta_c for k=3 (c=1,2,3) and k=4 (c=1..4) agree within combined 2 sigma.

\paragraph{Evaluation for the noisy-pure ensemble.}
Lemma 4 is a statement about a fixed state. Averaging it over the
ensemble of Section~2.5, \(\rho = (1-q)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi| + q I/2^n\)
with \(|\psi\rangle\) Haar, requires only the second and third Haar
moments of Pauli expectations, \(\mathbb{E}[\langle P\rangle^2] = 1/(d+1)\)
and \(\mathbb{E}[\langle P_u\rangle\langle P_s\rangle\langle P_{s'}\rangle]
= 2/((d+1)(d+2))\) when \(P_sP_{s'} = P_u\), together with four counts
over compatible Pauli pairs. Writing \(d = 2^n\), \(u = 1-q\), and
\(p_2 = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho^2) = u^2 + q(2-q)/d\),
\[
\zeta_1 = 4^{-n}\!\left[\,1
+ \frac{u^2(10^n-1)}{d+1}
+ \frac{2u^2(4^n-1)}{d+1}
+ \frac{2u^3\!\left(16^n - 10^n - 2\cdot 4^n + 2\right)}{(d+1)(d+2)}
\right] - p_2^{\,2},
\]
\[
\zeta_2 = 4^{-n}\!\left[\,28^n + \frac{u^2\!\left(34^n - 28^n\right)}{d+1}\right] - p_2^{\,2}.
\]
%% [V-EX] counts verified at n=2,3,4: sum over compatible pairs of
%% 3^|overlap| = 16^n with diagonal 10^n; of 9^|overlap| = 34^n with
%% diagonal 28^n. Haar moments verified numerically.
%% [V-ENS] both forms reproduce the ensemble-averaged exact evaluator
%% within 2 SEM at n = 3,4,5,6,7.

The four counts are elementary. Each qubit contributes one identity of
weight \(3^0\) and three Paulis of weight \(3^1\), giving
\(\sum_s 3^{|s|} = 10^n\); the compatible pairs give \(16^n\) with the
same weighting and \(34^n\) with its square, whose diagonals are \(10^n\)
and \(28^n\).

\paragraph{The threshold is analytic.}
Both expressions have a dominant term and geometrically smaller
corrections. In \(\zeta_2\), the leading \(4^{-n}28^n = 7^n\) is exact and
the correction grows only as \((34/8)^n = 4.25^n\), so \(\zeta_2/7^n \to 1\)
with no free constant. In \(\zeta_1\), the \(10^n\) term gives
\(4^{-n}10^n/2^n = (5/4)^n\) and the \(16^n\) term tends to the constant
\(2u^3\), so
\[
\zeta_1 \longrightarrow u^2\left(\tfrac{5}{4}\right)^{\! n} + 2u^3 - u^4,
\qquad
\zeta_2 \longrightarrow 7^n .
\]
Therefore
\[
M^*(n) = \frac{\zeta_2}{2\zeta_1}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\frac{1}{2(1-q)^2}\left(\frac{28}{5}\right)^{\! n},
\qquad \frac{28}{5} = 5.6,
\]
with both the base and the prefactor exact. At \(q = 0.1\) the prefactor
is \(0.6173\).
%% [V-EX] M*/5.6^n from the closed forms: 0.542 (n=8), 0.552 (n=9),
%% 0.562 (n=10), 0.579 (n=12), 0.610 (n=20), approaching 0.6173.
%% [V-EX] zeta_2/7^n: 1.0047 (n=10), 1.00004 (n=20).

The approach is slow because the constant \(2u^3 - u^4\) in \(\zeta_1\) is
still an eleventh of it at \(n=9\). This is what produces the window
dependence of the fitted base reported below, and the closed forms
reproduce that dependence: fitting them over \(n = 2\) to \(7\), \(2\) to
\(9\), and \(4\) to \(9\) returns bases \(5.18\), \(5.36\), and \(5.60\).
The drift is a finite-size property of the fit, and \(28/5\) is what it
drifts toward.
%% [V-EX] closed-form window refits 5.181 / 5.362 / 5.601 against the
%% exact per-state refits 5.143 / 5.339 / 5.591 and the sampled 5.34.

What is prior work here is the second-moment identity and the variance
decomposition it feeds. What we add is the evaluation for a physically
motivated ensemble, and the observation that the ratio of the two
projection variances — which separate upper bounds on them cannot
constrain — has a closed form whose growth base is \(28/5\).

A weight-only quadratic form is insufficient, and
Section~\ref{sec:zeta-closed-form} identifies exactly what it omits. The
weight-only form is the diagonal of an exact cubic identity already
available in \cite{huang2020shadows}, and the residual it leaves is that

the state that no form quadratic in the Pauli expectations can carry, and
it is present before any question of entanglement or correlation arises.

\paragraph{The prefactor is asymptotic, not finite-\(n\).}
The projection variances have closed forms for the ensemble studied here
(Section~\ref{sec:zeta-closed-form}), and \(M^*(n)\) is analytic for it.
What does not follow is a closed form for an arbitrary state: the
evaluation uses the Haar moments of the ensemble, and for a general
\(\rho\) the identity of \cite{huang2020shadows} must be summed over the
state's Pauli spectrum. The asymptotic base and prefactor are exact, but
they are approached slowly, and at the sizes reached here the finite-\(n\)
value of \(M^*(n)/5.6^n\) is still some fifteen percent below its limit.
