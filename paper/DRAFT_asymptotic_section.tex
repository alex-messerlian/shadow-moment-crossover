\subsection{What the fitted base converges to}
\label{sec:zeta1-closed-form}

The base fitted above drifts with the fitting window, from \(5.14\) over
\(n = 2\) to \(7\) to \(5.60\) over \(n = 4\) to \(9\). That drift is the
finite-size approach to a constant we can derive, and deriving it
requires the closed form for \(\zeta_1\) that Section~3.4 declined to
supply. The obstruction there was not that no closed form exists. It was
that the closed form is cubic in the Pauli expectations where the ansatz
was quadratic.

Write \(P_s\) for the Pauli string indexed by \(s \in \{I,X,Y,Z\}^n\),
let \(\mathrm{supp}(s)\) be the qubits on which \(s\) is not the
identity, and write \(\langle P_s \rangle = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho P_s)\).
Call an ordered pair \((s,s')\) \emph{compatible} when \(s\) and \(s'\)
agree on \(\mathrm{supp}(s) \cap \mathrm{supp}(s')\), and for a
compatible pair let \(s \triangle s'\) be the string equal to \(s\) on
\(\mathrm{supp}(s) \setminus \mathrm{supp}(s')\), equal to \(s'\) on
\(\mathrm{supp}(s') \setminus \mathrm{supp}(s)\), and identity
elsewhere. Then for the local Haar shadow of Section~2.1 and any
\(n\)-qubit state,
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{Tr}(G\rho)^2\right]
= 4^{-n} \sum_{(s,s')\ \mathrm{compatible}}
3^{\,|\mathrm{supp}(s)\, \cap\, \mathrm{supp}(s')|}\,
\langle P_s \rangle \langle P_{s'} \rangle
\langle P_{s \triangle s'} \rangle ,
\]
with \(\zeta_1 = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Tr}(G\rho)^2] - \mathrm{Tr}(\rho^2)^2\).
The identity follows from expanding the snapshot as
\(G = 2^{-n} \bigotimes_q (I + 3\,\hat n_q \!\cdot\! \vec\sigma)\) and
averaging in two stages. The measurement outcome enters linearly through
sign variables \(\varepsilon_q = \pm 1\), so squaring and summing over
them retains only those triples whose supports satisfy
\(\mathrm{supp}(u) = \mathrm{supp}(s) \triangle \mathrm{supp}(s')\);
every qubit then carries either zero or two factors of the measurement
axis, never one or three. Averaging the axes over the sphere contributes
\(\delta/3\) on each qubit of \(\mathrm{supp}(s) \cup \mathrm{supp}(s')\),
which forces agreement on the overlap and cancels against
\(3^{|s|+|s'|}\), leaving \(3^{|s \wedge s'|}\).

The identity is exact for every state, entangled or not, and reduces to
the single-qubit law of Section~3.3: at \(n=1\) the four contributing
pairs give \(\tfrac{1}{4}(1 + 3t^2 + t^2 + t^2) = \tfrac14 + \tfrac54 t^2\).
%% [V-EX] n=1 reduction exact. [V-CC] agrees to 1e-15 across the Bloch range.
An independent evaluation, by exact single-qubit Clifford 3-design
average and exact outcome enumeration rather than by the estimator of
Section~3.1, reproduces the sampled \(\zeta_1\) at every system size
within its Monte Carlo spread.
%% [V-CC] n=2..8, deviations 0.25%, 0.03%, -0.04%, -0.21%.

Two consequences follow.

\paragraph{The rejected ansatz is exactly the diagonal.}
Restricting the sum to \(s = s'\) gives
\(4^{-n} \sum_s 3^{|s|} \langle P_s \rangle^2\), which is precisely the
weight-only quadratic form of Section~3.4 with \(c_w = 3^w/4^n\). The
residual that form leaves is therefore not an unidentified shortfall but
exactly the off-diagonal sum. Fitting free weight coefficients to the
diagonal recovers \(3^w/4^n\) to \(9 \times 10^{-16}\), and fitting the
same form to the exact \(\zeta_1\) across the nineteen-state family of
Section~3.4 leaves a maximum relative residual of \(0.122\), reproducing
the figure reported there.
%% [V-CC] c_w recovery 8.9e-16; ansatz residual 0.122 vs committed 0.123.

\paragraph{The diagonal fixes the base.}
Two exact facts settle the diagonal's growth. First,
\(\sum_s 3^{|s|} = (1 + 3\cdot 3)^n = 10^n\), each qubit contributing one
identity of weight \(3^0\) and three Paulis of weight \(3^1\). Second,
for a Haar-random pure state depolarized at rate \(q\), the mean squared
Pauli expectation over non-identity strings is \((1-q)^2/(2^n+1)\)
exactly. Since \(10^n/(4^n 2^n) = (5/4)^n\), the diagonal grows as
\((1-q)^2 (5/4)^n\).
%% [V-EX] sum verified 100/1000/10000 at n=2,3,4; mean <P>^2 ratio 1.0000.
%% [V-CC] DIAG/(5/4)^n = 0.589, 0.794, 0.747, 0.796, 0.796, 0.809, 0.806 (n=2..8).
%% [V-HI] 0.788, 0.799, 0.8025 at n=5,6,7 against (1-q)^2 = 0.81.

The off-diagonal sum is bounded. Its terms carry three non-identity
Pauli expectations; the two compatible factors commute and multiply to
the third, so the Haar third moment is \(2/((d+1)(d+2))\) with
\(d = 2^n\), while the compatible pairs carry total weight \(16^n\) of
which \(10^n\) is the diagonal. The off-diagonal therefore tends to
\(2(1-q)^3\), approached from below with corrections of order \(2^{-n}\)
and \((5/8)^n\).
%% [V-EX] weights 16^n and 10^n exact at n=2,3,4.
%% [V-EX] Haar third moment ratio 1.019, 0.986, 0.998 at n=2,3,4.
%% [V-HI] measured/predicted ratio 1.044, 1.030, 1.002 at n=5,6,7
%%        (300/120/30 states); at n=7, 1.0019 +/- 0.012.
%% [PEND] PASS 6 rerun on committed seeds at high state count.

Since the off-diagonal is bounded while the diagonal grows
geometrically, the diagonal dominates and
\[
\zeta_1(n) \;\longrightarrow\;
(1-q)^2 \left(\tfrac{5}{4}\right)^{\! n} + 2(1-q)^3 - (1-q)^4 .
\]
At \(q = 0.1\) this is \(0.81\,(1.25)^n + 0.802\), which matches the
sampled \(\zeta_1\) to \(1.2\%\) at \(n = 8\) and \(0.5\%\) at \(n = 9\).
%% [V-CC] +1.18% at n=8, -0.51% at n=9. Low n is off, as an asymptotic
%%        form should be.

The second projection variance has base \(7\) per qubit, the exact value
at a maximally mixed marginal recorded in Section~2.5 and one that
Haar-typical states approach; the sampled ratio \(\zeta_2/7^n\) is
constant to \(1.3\%\) over \(n \ge 6\).
%% [MEAS] C_2 = 1.018 +/- 0.014 over n>=6. Measured, not derived.
Hence
\[
M^*(n) = \frac{\zeta_2}{2\zeta_1}
\;\longrightarrow\; \frac{C_2}{2(1-q)^2}
\left(\frac{28}{5}\right)^{\! n},
\qquad \frac{28}{5} = 5.6 .
\]

The window dependence reported above is the approach to this constant,
not an instability in the fit. Three diagnostics agree, none of which
depends on a choice of fitting window:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
geometric-mean ratio, \(n = 4 \ldots 9\) & predicted & measured \\
\hline
\(\zeta_1(n+1)/\zeta_1(n)\) & \(5/4 = 1.25\) & \(1.2433\) \\
\(\zeta_2(n+1)/\zeta_2(n)\) & \(7\)          & \(6.9732\) \\
\(M^*(n+1)/M^*(n)\)         & \(28/5 = 5.6\) & \(5.6087\) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The \(M^*\) figure carries a \(95\%\) interval of \([5.457,\, 5.746]\),
which contains \(5.6\), and \(M^*(n)/5.6^n\) is flat over \(n \ge 4\),
running \(0.557\) at \(n = 4\) to \(0.562\) at \(n = 9\).

The base is therefore derived. The prefactor is not. It carries \(C_2\),
which we measure rather than derive, and the finite-size values of
\(M^*(n)/5.6^n\) sit below the asymptotic \(C_2/2(1-q)^2 \approx 0.62\)
because the constant term in \(\zeta_1\) is still \(12\%\) of it at
\(n = 9\). What this section establishes is the growth rate of the
threshold, not its absolute size at any particular \(n\), for which the
evaluated coefficients of Section~3.5 remain the operative input.

A weight-only quadratic form is insufficient, and
Section~\ref{sec:zeta1-closed-form} identifies exactly what it omits: it
is the diagonal of an exact cubic identity, and the residual it leaves
is that identity's off-diagonal sum. The shortfall is not an unresolved
feature of the algebra. For \(n \ge 2\) the projection variances are
still estimated numerically in what follows, since evaluating the exact
form requires the full Pauli spectrum; the variance law is exact and its
inputs, as evaluated here, are sampled.


% ============================ BLOCK C =================================
% Patch to the conclusion. Present text opens the three questions with

Two questions follow from this work, the first of the three we opened
having been closed in Section~\ref{sec:zeta1-closed-form}: \(\zeta_1\)
does admit a closed form, cubic rather than quadratic in the Pauli
expectations, and it fixes the growth base of \(M^*(n)\) at \(28/5\).
What it does not fix is the prefactor, which carries the second
projection variance's constant and which we measure rather than derive.
